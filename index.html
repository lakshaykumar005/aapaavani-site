Three decisions we'd get right before this becomes a final design

These are the three calls we want on record before a single wall gets poured — each one earned from what actually goes wrong on site, not from a textbook.

01 — Technology choice

Build the membrane in once. Don't pay to add it twice.

A conventional ASP or SBR looks cheaper today. But tertiary membrane filtration ahead of discharge is very likely to become a mandatory requirement soon, not an optional upgrade. Retrofitting a membrane stage into a plant that wasn't built for one typically costs close to double what it takes to integrate it from day one. Going with MBR now isn't the premium option — it's the version that never has to be rebuilt.

02 — Screening

The plant is only as good as what never reaches it.

Every blower, diffuser, pump and membrane downstream depends on debris never getting past the first stage. We'd specify a mechanised, automatic fine screen at the inlet rather than a manual bar screen — so an unattended night shift or a monsoon surge doesn't become the moment something expensive gets damaged. The offer already carries a rotary drum screen at 100 m³/hr, 2–4 mm aperture, in SS 304.

automatic fine screen
03 — Tank depth

Five metres, not seven.

The tentative plan runs the aeration tanks close to seven metres of water depth. Every extra metre adds hydrostatic pressure the diffusers have to push air against — which lowers oxygen transfer efficiency, wears diffuser membranes faster, and pushes the blower selection into a higher-pressure, higher-energy category. Servicing diffusers at that depth is also a genuinely harder maintenance job. Five metres keeps oxygen transfer efficient and diffuser access practical for the life of the plant.

The technical case

Why membrane bioreactor, specifically for a medical campus

These are the reasons this decision is different for an institution training doctors than it would be for a hotel or an apartment complex.

Pathogen and AMR barrier

Medical college wastewater carries a higher load of antibiotic residues and resistant organisms than ordinary domestic sewage. A conventional activated sludge process relies mainly on biological degradation and settling, both of which allow a fraction of resistant cells to pass through in the final effluent. The MBR membrane is a physical barrier below the size of bacteria — it retains that biomass mechanically, independent of how the biology performs on a given day. For a campus that trains doctors, this is the argument that actually matters, not just water clarity.

Footprint

The full treatment building, from bar screen to treated water tank, fits inside an envelope of roughly 36 m by 21.4 m — under 780 sqm — because the membrane replaces the secondary clarifier and lets the biological tanks run at a higher biomass concentration. A conventional ASP sized for the same 1.5 MLD load, with a separate clarifier and larger aeration volume, would need meaningfully more land than your campus has to give. Across the ESTAPRO™ range we size MBR plants at roughly half the footprint of an equivalent conventional STP.

Reuse quality

MBR permeate is consistently low enough in suspended solids and turbidity to be reused directly for gardens, flushing, or cooling makeup water without a separate tertiary filtration stage — a real operating cost saving over the life of the plant, and it is exactly the treated water tank shown in the drawing that will feed that reuse line.

Odour and neighbours

A properly designed and covered system — with the bar screen and oil and grease chambers kept as sealed open-type units with removable covers per the drawing notes, and controlled aeration through the tank layout — essentially eliminates the odour complaints that come with an overloaded or poorly operated conventional system.

Compliance record

Consistent effluent quality, not just occasional grab-sample compliance, is what protects an institution during a pollution control board inspection or an NABH or NMC review. This is the difference between a system built to pass a spot check and one built to actually run clean every day.

The process

From bar screen to reusable permeate

Treatment begins with a bar screen to remove coarse solids, followed by an oil & grease chamber for the separation of oil, grease and floating scum. The wastewater is collected in the collection tank, which provides flow equalization before entering the anoxic tank for denitrification. It then passes through the drum screen to remove fine suspended solids before flowing into the aeration tank, where aerobic micro-organisms degrade organic pollutants.

The mixed liquor is then treated in the MBR tank, where submerged membrane modules produce high-quality treated water by retaining biomass and suspended solids. The permeate is drawn by permeate pumps, while the backwash system periodically cleans the membranes to maintain filtration efficiency. After disinfectant dosing, treated water is stored in the treated water tank for reuse or safe discharge. Excess sludge is transferred to the sludge holding tank and sent to the dewatering system for volume reduction and disposal.

At 1.5 MLD, the KVG plant would be the second-largest MBR sewage treatment plant we have built — behind only the 1.6 MLD municipal plant at Karwar City Municipal Corporation — and the largest we have built for an educational or healthcare institution.

Mission

“To design, re-engineer, and automate wastewater treatment systems that deliver efficient performance, high reuse, and consistent compliance through intelligent design and process control.”

Vision

“To become the most trusted partner for industrial smart wastewater treatment systems, setting the standard for reliable, automated, and performance-led engineering infrastructure.”

Our approach is to diagnose why treatment systems fail, then design the recovery — including pilot testing before full deployment. We want plants that behave as predictable, controllable systems, not as trial-and-error daily fire-fights. That is the same standard we would bring to KVG.

The platforms

Your plant is an ESTAPRO™ MBR STP

We build to two standardised product platforms rather than designing every plant from a blank sheet. That is what keeps delivery predictable and spares interchangeable across the fleet.

ESTAPRO™ — sewage treatment

Domestic & institutional STPs

State-of-the-art MBR sewage treatment plants, customised to site, in civil-based or container-based configurations. Built for facilities struggling with poor treatment performance, odour, high operating cost and frequent breakdowns.

  • 10 KLD to several MLD — the KVG plant sits at the top of this range
  • Up to 50 % less space than a conventional STP
  • Micro/ultrafiltration combined with biological treatment
  • Online sensors, automated dosing and smart dashboards
  • Retrofit capability — upgrades an underperforming plant without full reconstruction
  • Meets CPCB and State Pollution Control Board discharge standards
Hospitals & healthcare Educational campuses Townships & hotels
AUSTENPRO™ — effluent treatment

Industrial ETPs

Customised effluent treatment plants for industries and factories. Extensive treatability studies establish the parameters to be treated, then the unit is designed specifically against them — analysis, design, fabrication, installation and commissioning all in house, with periodic maintenance and health checks afterwards.

  • Tailor-made to the measured effluent, not a catalogue selection
  • In-house team across the whole chain, one accountable party
  • Zero liquid discharge integration where the process demands it
  • Periodic maintenance and plant health check-ups after handover
